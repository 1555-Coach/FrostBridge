# FrostBridge training video production package — v0.1
**Status:** Draft for adult FIRST LEGO League volunteers. Require FIRST/event approval before public release. **Format:** 16:9 educational presentation, visible AI instructor on intro, transitions and youth-protection segment. Aim 10 minutes. Use official FrostBridge blue logo and E3RC color scheme.

## Presenter/audio direction
A calm, approachable professional adult presenter, no simulated FIRST representative. Plain American English, moderate conversational pace. Presenter should appear in the right third of the frame with subtitles not obscuring text. Background music optional and low during introductory/closing title only, off during detailed instruction and safety guidance. Offer a captioned version and transcript; avoid flashing transitions.

## Slide-by-slide narration draft (adapt for measured timing)
### 1. Welcome (50–60 seconds)
“Welcome to FrostBridge, a student-led accessibility initiative supported by E3 Robotics Center and FIRST Team 1555 CryoFrost. This short training will help adult volunteers make FIRST LEGO League events more welcoming for children, families, and adults with disabilities or support needs that might not be immediately visible. You are not expected to diagnose anyone or act as a clinician. Your job is to listen, provide practical assistance within your event role, and connect visitors to the right people when needed. Our goal is a safe, respectful and enjoyable event for everyone.”

### 2. Needs are not always visible (55–65 seconds)
“People may experience sensory sensitivity, communication differences, chronic conditions, anxiety in crowded environments, or other needs that you cannot see. There is no single appearance or behavior that proves a disability. A visitor may tell you what helps, or may simply request a quieter area or written instructions. Accept the request at face value. Never ask someone to prove they have a disability or identify a diagnosis. Some events may offer an optional identifier, but support must never depend on one. The same support process applies whether or not a visitor chooses to use it.”

### 3. FIRST values in action (45–55 seconds)
“FIRST Core Values guide how we work together: Discovery, Innovation, Impact, Inclusion, Teamwork and Fun. During an event, Inclusion means welcoming different ways of participating. Teamwork means asking what a person needs instead of making assumptions. Innovation and Impact mean changing our setup when it creates unnecessary barriers. Gracious Professionalism means being courteous and capable even when the event is busy. Support should preserve participants’ dignity and must never change judging expectations on an individual volunteer’s authority.”

### 4. Ask, Listen, Offer, Connect (65–75 seconds)
“Four steps will help in most situations. First: Ask. Try, ‘What would help you right now?’ Second: Listen. Give someone time to answer; speak directly to them, not over them. Third: Offer only what is actually available—for example, clear written directions, a little more time, or a confirmed quieter area. Fourth: Connect. If a request is outside your role, discreetly connect with the event’s designated support or safety lead. Avoid saying ‘you don’t look disabled’ or ‘everyone else manages.’ Instead say, ‘Thank you for telling me. Let’s see what works.’”

### 5. Supporting children safely (75–90 seconds)
“Youth protection comes first. FIRST’s Youth Protection Program and event safeguarding rules apply to every interaction with a minor. Never take a child alone into a quiet room, hallway, vehicle or secluded area. Keep interactions observable and interruptible and follow your event’s authorized supervision process. Involve the child’s responsible adult, coach, or designated event lead as appropriate. Never privately contact a child through a personal phone, text message, social account or email. Do not touch a child or their mobility aids without permission, except as authorized under applicable emergency procedures. If a child is distressed, remain calm, reduce unnecessary demands, and bring in the proper adult. Report youth-protection concerns through official channels, not through a FrostBridge feedback form.”

### 6. Sensory accommodations (55–65 seconds)
“Robotics tournaments can be exciting and loud. Cheering, announcements, crowds, lights and queues may overwhelm some visitors. Do not assume that a distressed child is misbehaving. Ask how to help. If a quieter area is available, follow the event’s approved directions and supervision process. Never promise a quiet room that has not been confirmed, and never treat the space as unsupervised child care or isolation. Volunteers must know the location, route, supervision procedures and who to contact before directing visitors there.”

### 7. Supporting adults (55–65 seconds)
“Adults may identify their own needs and preferences. Respect what they say without seeking a diagnosis. Address the adult directly, even if they are accompanied by family or a support person, unless they request another arrangement. Ask whether written directions, less noise, extra processing time, or another available option would be helpful. Do not touch, follow, or escort someone without consent. Respect a ‘no thank you.’ Share information only with event staff who genuinely need it to help resolve the request.”

### 8. Practice scenarios (65–75 seconds)
“Consider three common situations. First, a young participant covers their ears and asks for somewhere quiet. Offer a confirmed quieter option and keep approved adult supervision; do not lead the child away alone. Second, an adult at registration says the crowd is overwhelming. Ask if written directions or an approved alternate wait location would help. Third, a child takes a long pause before answering a judging question. Give them processing time rather than speaking for them or labeling the behavior. If a request may affect official judging procedure, refer it to the correct event lead.”

### 9. Protecting privacy (55–65 seconds)
“Privacy is essential. FrostBridge volunteers do not record names, diagnoses, individual badge use, medical conditions or identifiable accommodation requests. Do not post photos, detailed stories or sensitive disclosures to GitHub, Slack, volunteer chats or social media. You may record anonymous operational observations such as ‘signage was hard to find,’ but not who needed assistance. Safety, medical and youth-protection incidents must be handled through official FIRST and venue reporting procedures. FrostBridge is not a substitute for those systems.”

### 10. Review and close (50–60 seconds)
“Before your shift, make sure you can answer four questions. Can people ask for support without an identifier or diagnosis? Yes. Can you take a child alone to a quiet room? No. Should you log someone's disability in a volunteer chat? No. And whom do you contact for an accommodation or safety concern? Contact the event’s designated support or safety lead. Remember: Ask. Listen. Offer. Connect. Thank you for helping FrostBridge make STEM events more welcoming.”

## Reusable video review before release
This video is designed to be reused across FIRST LEGO League events. Keep event dates, venue details, named contacts, and local directions out of the narration and slides. Each event should share its current support contacts, quiet-area directions, and supervision procedures separately through its own briefing materials.

Before release, review the guidance against current FIRST Youth Protection Program requirements and the official FIRST LEGO League volunteer manual. Include an accessibility review by people with lived experience, then confirm the final runtime and captions.
