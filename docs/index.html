FrostBridge

Bridging understanding. Building inclusion.

Volunteer training and practical support resources for community events.

Draft for the November FLL pilot. All volunteers must use confirmed event-specific contacts, locations and safeguarding procedures. This training does not replace venue policies or required safety training.

10-minute volunteer training

1 minute: Understand

Not every disability or support need is visible. Guests may need more time, a quieter environment, clearer directions or another way to communicate.

2 minutes: Respond

Welcome. Ask: “What would help you right now?” Listen. Offer available support or contact the event lead.

1 minute: Privacy

Never ask for a diagnosis, proof, or an identifier. Any badge is optional. Do not assume what a person needs.

2 minutes: Practical help

Use short, clear directions; allow time; offer written information; provide directions to quieter space only if confirmed.

2 minutes: Practice

Someone asks for a quiet place. Someone needs longer to answer at registration. Practice your response and find the designated lead.

2 minutes: Safety and recap

Ask permission before touching. Respect privacy. Follow child protection and emergency plans. Know your escalation contact.

Volunteer quick reference

ASK · LISTEN · HELP.

“What would help you right now?” Be patient. Offer choices. Never require an identifier or ask for a diagnosis. Contact the event accessibility lead when needed.

Before the tournament

  • Confirm venue accessibility lead and method to contact them.
  • Confirm any available quieter space, directions and supervision rules.
  • Approve and print visitor-facing signage and guidance.
  • Walk the visitor route and rehearse two real-world scenarios.

Visitor information

Robotics tournaments may include cheering, announcements, bright displays and crowds. You can ask a volunteer for assistance without explaining your disability. Event-specific quiet-space arrangements and entrances will be published after confirmation.
