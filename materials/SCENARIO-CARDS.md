# FrostBridge: 5 adult-volunteer scenario cards

Use these as short role-plays after the 10-minute briefing. Adult trainers review correct responses. Do not act out or label a real child's disability.

## A. Cheering is too loud for a child
A child with a coach says: “Can I go somewhere quieter?”
**Say:** “Yes, let’s find the event-approved quieter spot. Coach, could you come with us or work with the accommodation lead?”
**Do:** Confirm room, supervision and safe route. Never lead a child away alone.

## B. Adult asks to wait elsewhere
A spectator says: “I can't stand in this crowded line.”
**Say:** “I can ask our event lead what alternate check-in options we have. Would you like written directions?”
**Do:** Avoid seeking a reason or documenting the person's name/disability.

## C. Child does not answer judging question immediately
A child pauses and looks away.
**Say:** “Take your time.” Avoid speaking for the child, reducing score, labeling behavior, or changing official competition rules yourself. Consult judge advisor/accommodation lead if a request requires procedural decisions.

## D. Another volunteer asks what a wristband means
**Say:** “It's an optional signal that someone may appreciate patience or support. It isn't a diagnosis. Everyone can ask for assistance with or without it.”
**Do:** No profiling, badge scans or social media photos.

## E. Child appears distressed without a responsible adult in sight
**Say:** “You're safe here with us. Would you like help finding your coach or trusted adult?”
**Do:** Stay in a public, observable area and promptly engage another authorized volunteer and the event safety/contact lead. Follow the official lost-child and youth-protection protocol.

### Debrief prompts
Did we ask rather than assume? Was the contact safe and observable? Did we respect privacy? Was the offered support real and available? Did we know where to escalate?
